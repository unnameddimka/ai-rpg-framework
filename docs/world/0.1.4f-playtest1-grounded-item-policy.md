# Mallowstead 0.1.4f-playtest1 — Authored Grounded-Item Policy

## Status

Implementation specification for Mallowstead authored world content.

Engine/protocol/editor behavior is specified in `docs/engine/0.1.4f-playtest1-grounded-item-policy-throughput-ui.md`.

Base: `0.1.4e-candidate3`.

Target version: `0.1.4f-playtest1`.

Apply the same non-secret grounded-item policy to both public and private Mallowstead authored world sources. Regenerate generated world artifacts through the existing pipeline.

---

# 1. Design intent

Mallowstead does **not** attempt to mechanically model every physical object that may appear in prose.

Ordinary scene details may remain narrative props. Mara may pour incidental chamomile tea, characters may use ordinary untracked household clutter, and narration may create mundane details when no gameplay mechanic depends on their identity/state.

A smaller authored set of semantic item categories is mechanically grounded because Mallowstead gameplay depends on those objects being created, possessed, transferred, consumed, equipped, traded, unlocked, offered, or otherwise changed through engine mechanics.

The world-level grounded-item policy tells Character models where this boundary lies.

---

# 2. Mallowstead authoring invariant

For Mallowstead:

> If a class of item has gameplay-significant engine mechanics, its semantic category must be covered by `groundedItemPolicy`.

The engine does not automatically check this invariant.

Adding a grounded category means characters may no longer invent missing instances from that category through narration. Therefore authored mechanics/items must exist whenever gameplay expects characters to obtain or manipulate such an object.

If a later feature makes a previously narrative object mechanically important, update this policy in the same world-content change.

Example: if Harlan later pays wages in nails and nails become formal inventory/reward items, add nails to the grounded policy in that candidate.

---

# 3. Required top-level property

Add a non-empty top-level string:

```json
"groundedItemPolicy": "..."
```

Use equivalent text in `data/world.json` and `data/world.private.json`.

The statement must remain broad enough not to disclose secret concrete objects.

---

# 4. Required grounded semantic territory

The Mallowstead policy must cover the following categories.

## 4.1 Keys and access-control items

Ground all keys and comparable portable access-control objects.

Reason: locks/doors/containers use formal key mechanics.

Do not enumerate which secret keys or secret locks exist.

## 4.2 Clothing and jewelry

Ground clothing, wearable garments, jewelry, ornaments intended as canonical items, and comparable authored wearable valuables.

Reason: clothing/equipment and jewelry ownership/identity may be canonical, and broad jewelry wording protects hidden ornament/medallion content without naming it.

Do not enumerate secret jewelry or medallions.

## 4.3 Magical, supernatural, and special interactive objects/devices

Ground magical objects, supernatural objects, artifacts, and special devices whose behavior is authored as a mechanic.

This broad category covers things such as the Memory Stone or arcane study device without revealing that any particular undiscovered secret artifact exists.

Do not list secret instance IDs or secret definitions.

## 4.4 Tavern serving vessels

Ground the tavern's mechanically modeled:

- mugs;
- bowls;
- plates;
- other explicitly authored reusable serving vessels if later added to the same serving mechanics.

These objects currently participate in formal inventory/fill/serve/return workflows and therefore must not be invented, picked up, handed over, placed, filled, or returned purely through narration.

Ordinary incidental cups/dishes outside the mechanically authored tavern serving system may still be narrative props if the scene needs them and they are clearly not standing in for a grounded tavern item.

## 4.5 Authored tavern food/drink and other formal food offerings

Ground specific food/drink portions that exist as canonical items and participate in formal food mechanics.

This includes the authored tavern servings and canonical edible offerings that can participate in Chuhaister-related food mechanics.

This does **not** make all food in fiction grounded.

An incidental sandwich, herbal tea, snack, or meal detail that is not an engine-supplied item and does not stand in for a grounded category may remain narrative.

The model must not narratively invent a canonical edible offering in order to trigger or satisfy a formal food mechanic.

## 4.6 Potions, salves, ointments, and authored remedies

Ground potions, salves, ointments, tonics, medicines/remedies, and comparable authored medicinal goods intended to exist as canonical inventory/trade/reward objects.

This includes Mara's authored salves/potions.

## 4.7 Maksym's authored merchandise and trade goods

Ground the authored merchandise/trade-good categories represented in Maksym's formal stock and economy.

This covers the canonical town/regional goods he actually sells or trades through the engine, including writing supplies and other authored merchandise.

Do not interpret this as `all conceivable manufactured objects are grounded`. A mundane object not represented by Maksym's formal merchandise and not covered elsewhere may still be a narrative prop.

Squirrel pelts and other explicitly engine-traded goods are grounded when they participate in formal trade even if they are goods sold **to** Maksym rather than goods originating in his stock.

## 4.8 Farm-work reward produce and household food

Ground the canonical vegetables, grain/groats, fruit, eggs, cheese, bread, and other food/produce instances that the engine may issue as farmer work rewards.

The current category includes the authored reward families represented by:

- turnips;
- onions;
- buckwheat groats;
- apples;
- eggs;
- farm cheese;
- bread loaves.

This category protects the mechanic, not the nouns globally. Incidental narrative food outside the authored reward/formal-food system remains allowed.

---

# 5. Categories intentionally **not** globally grounded

Do not ground broad categories merely because one specific canonical instance may exist.

In particular, do not globally reserve:

- all tools;
- all weapons;
- all household objects;
- all furniture;
- all food/drink;
- all cups/containers everywhere;
- all books/papers unless they fall under an explicitly grounded mechanic/category;
- every object mentioned in character descriptions or location prose.

For example, Harlan may narratively handle ordinary smithing tools or Maksym may narratively reference ordinary weapon/road gear when no formal mechanic depends on those invented objects.

If the engine explicitly supplies a particular canonical tool/weapon instance in a scene, that specific instance remains grounded by the generic engine rule even though `tools` or `weapons` are not globally reserved categories.

---

# 6. Secret-content rule

The policy must protect secrets through **category abstraction**, not through secret enumeration.

Good:

> Jewelry and magical or supernatural items are mechanically grounded.

Bad:

> The Iron Medallion, the key to its glazed frame, and [other hidden object] are grounded.

The Character model may learn that the world mechanically tracks jewelry or magical objects. It must not learn from this statement which secret examples actually exist.

This is an intentional tradeoff: broad semantic territory is reserved to the engine so the model cannot casually invent a colliding secret-class object.

---

# 7. Recommended authored statement

Use wording with the following semantics. Minor editorial polishing is acceptable, but do not weaken the distinctions.

```text
ITEM GROUNDING IN MALLOWSTEAD

Most incidental physical details may exist as narrative props and can be freely introduced or handled in narration when no engine mechanic depends on them.

The following semantic categories are mechanically grounded and reserved to the engine: keys and portable access-control items; clothing and jewelry; magical, supernatural, artifact, and other special interactive items/devices; the tavern's mechanically modeled mugs, bowls, plates, and formal food/drink servings; potions, salves, ointments, tonics, and other authored remedies; authored merchandise and trade goods handled by Maksym's economy, including explicitly traded goods; and the canonical vegetables, grain/groats, fruit, eggs, cheese, bread, and other food/produce issued through farm-work rewards or otherwise used by formal food mechanics.

Do not invent a new instance of a grounded category or narrate acquiring, taking, giving, transferring, placing, filling, consuming, equipping, buying, selling, writing on, destroying, or otherwise changing a grounded item unless the engine supplies the corresponding canonical item and formal action/result. If a grounded item is mentioned but is absent from your current accessible items or available formal actions, that means you cannot currently manipulate it; it does not become a narrative prop.

Any concrete canonical item instance explicitly supplied by the engine is also grounded even if its broad category is not listed above.

Ordinary incidental objects outside these grounded categories may remain narrative props. This may include things such as an incidental cup of chamomile tea, a sandwich, generic smithing clutter, ordinary utensils, cloths, or other scene details when they are not being used as substitutes for an engine-grounded item or mechanic.
```

The statement is model-facing world law. It is not player-facing lore and need not be shown in ordinary UI.

# 8. Garrick/Nell expected behavior

With this policy in force, the ale-service playtest scenario should be interpreted as follows:

1. Garrick possesses a canonical tavern mug.
2. Garrick formally performs `fill`.
3. The mug remains grounded and remains wherever canonical state says it remains.
4. Garrick may not narrate an additional grounded placement/transfer as if it completed.
5. Nell knows tavern mugs are a grounded category.
6. If the mug is absent from Nell's accessible items and no `take_item`/transfer action exists, Nell must conclude that she cannot yet take it.
7. Nell must wait for Garrick's actual formal transfer or choose another genuinely available action; she may not reinterpret the mug as an untracked prop.

The policy does not require deterministic engine parsing of either character's narration.

---

# 9. Narrative freedom examples

The following remain acceptable when consistent with character/location fiction and when they do not substitute for a grounded mechanic:

- Mara pours herself an incidental cup of chamomile tea.
- Harlan wipes soot from a tool with an incidental scrap of cloth.
- A character eats a narratively incidental sandwich that is not a canonical edible item or Chuhaister offering.
- A room contains ordinary untracked clutter, utensils, bedding details, decorations, or similar props.

The following are not acceptable without formal grounding:

- inventing a spare room key;
- casually producing an untracked necklace when jewelry is reserved;
- inventing a magical charm/device;
- inventing a canonical tavern mug/plate/bowl to complete service;
- narratively creating a farm-reward onion or groats payout;
- narratively producing a potion/salve;
- inventing Maksym merchandise to buy/sell;
- inventing an edible canonical offering in order to summon Chuhaister.

---

# 10. Future authoring rule

Whenever Mallowstead gains a new mechanically significant item family, ask:

> Could a Character model otherwise invent or manipulate an instance of this semantic category as an ordinary narrative prop and thereby bypass the new mechanic?

If yes, extend `groundedItemPolicy` in the same authored-world change.

Examples:

- Harlan begins paying wages in canonical nails -> add nails / formal smithing stock as grounded semantic territory.
- A future quest tracks letters or documents mechanically -> ground the relevant document category if not already protected by the existing writing/trade wording.
- A new purely decorative narrative object with no gameplay state -> do not ground it merely because it exists in prose.

---

# 11. Tests/content checks

Add world-content assertions that:

1. public and private sources contain non-empty equivalent `groundedItemPolicy` text;
2. the text covers keys/access items;
3. clothing and jewelry;
4. magical/special objects;
5. tavern mugs/bowls/plates and formal servings;
6. potions/salves/remedies;
7. Maksym trade goods/economy goods;
8. farmer reward food/produce and formal-food items;
9. the text explicitly permits ordinary narrative props outside grounded categories;
10. the text explicitly says absence from accessible items/actions does not downgrade a grounded item into a narrative prop;
11. no secret concrete item/secret ID is named merely to explain grounding.

Do not add automatic semantic validation that every `itemDefinition` is covered by the prose statement.

---

# 12. Acceptance criteria

Mallowstead world content is correct when:

- the authored statement reserves every currently gameplay-significant item category that must not be bypassed by prose;
- ordinary incidental props remain narratively available;
- broad categories protect secret classes without disclosing hidden instances;
- a missing formal item/action means `unavailable`, never `safe to invent narratively`;
- future mechanics can expand grounding by editing authored world text rather than requiring a new engine ontology;
- public/private world content remains functionally aligned.
